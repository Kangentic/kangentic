# Relay transport

`src/main/mobile-bridge/transport/relay-client.ts` is the desktop's **outbound-only** WebSocket client to a blind relay (self-hostable, or Kangentic's hosted instance). The relay forwards opaque ciphertext frames only - it authenticates nothing and reads nothing, because every frame is already Noise-encrypted (or, during pairing, is itself a Noise handshake message the relay cannot decrypt).

- **Wire contract:** connect to `${relayUrl}?slot=<hex-encoded-slot-id>&role=desktop`. Both slot ids are *derived*: `derivePairingSlotId(token)` during pairing, and `deriveSessionSlotId(desktopKey, phoneKey)` for an ongoing session (both in `packages/protocol/src/crypto/slot.ts`, both 16 bytes / 32 hex characters). The relay rendezvouses the two connections presenting the *same* value and never interprets its cryptographic meaning, only its bytes. **Both slots are routing labels, never key material** - the slot rides in a query string, the most-logged part of a URL, so nothing secret may be placed there. `role` is a metrics hint only: the relay attributes its waiting-peer gauge by it, never gates on it, and collapses anything but the exact literal to `unknown` (`kangentic-relay`'s `guards/peerRole.ts`). **The relay server lives in the separate [`kangentic-relay`](https://github.com/Kangentic/kangentic-relay) repo**, which implements exactly this contract (see its README for the self-host quickstart and full config reference).
- **Node's stack first, Chromium's as the fallback.** The client dials with Node's global `WebSocket` (undici). In the app, `createTransport()` also hands it Electron's `net.WebSocket` (`relay-websocket.ts`) as a fallback stack, which honours the system proxy, PAC/WPAD and the OS certificate store where undici ignores all three. The fallback dials outright while `session.resolveProxy()` puts a proxy first for the relay (the first entry of its PAC-style answer decides, as it is the one Chromium tries first, so a list that leads with `DIRECT` dials on undici; cached for 60 s and refreshed in the background, so the first dial before an answer goes out on undici). Otherwise it gets a turn after two failed undici dials in a row, and once it opens where undici failed, the client stays on it for 30 minutes (`chooseWebSocket()` in `relay-client.ts`). That covers a TLS-inspecting firewall whose root CA is in the OS store but not in Node's bundled list. `relay-client.ts` itself imports nothing from Electron.
  - **Why undici first.** From October 2026 every dial went through `net.WebSocket`, and round trips got slow and erratic. A standalone Electron 44.5.1 rig dialed both stacks to the hosted relay in the same 30 s cycles, from the same machine, for two evening hours on Oct 7 (about 5,100 small echoes per stack in each of two rigs). The stacks came out close. Echo p99 was 647 and 656 ms on Chromium against 586 and 543 ms on undici (the two rigs), 18 and 16 echoes over 1 s against 14 and 14, worst cases 5.5 and 5.3 s against 2.0 and 4.2 s, and the dial tail was the same. Most of the tail hit both stacks in the same minutes, so it sits on the relay path; the relay task covers it. Chromium's own share is small. Its network log showed stalled bytes arriving late at the socket, never held inside Electron. Connections that negotiated Encrypted ClientHello (the hosted relay's zone publishes an ECH config in its DNS HTTPS record, and Chromium uses it; undici never does) had 0.94% of echoes over 500 ms against 0.50% in a rig with ECH turned off in the same hour. Chromium's slow dials finished TCP and TLS under 100 ms and then waited seconds for the upgrade reply. Undici is first because it is the stack every dial used before October 2026, it was never the worse one in the rig, and the isolated ~19 s dials in the desktop's logs appeared only after the switch away from it. The rig reproduced those during a relay rough patch (Oct 7, 22:49 to 23:01 UTC) on Chromium only: dials of 19.9 and 20.0 s and one 35 s dial timeout, while undici's worst in the same minutes was 10.7 s.
  - Measured on Electron 44.5.1: `binaryType` defaults to `'nodebuffer'` (the client sets `'arraybuffer'`); the handshake sends `Origin: http(s)://<relay host>`, which `kangentic-relay` never reads; a failed dial delivers a bare `error` Event and puts the `net::` error in the following close's `reason`; `close()` while connecting aborts with close code 1006 and no error event.
- **Reconnect with capped exponential backoff:** starts at 500ms, doubles up to a 30s ceiling, resets on a successful connect. The reconnect timer is armed BEFORE the `'reconnecting'` notification, so a listener that reacts by redialing or closing clears an armed timer instead of racing one.
- **`redialNow({ force?, reason? })`** (`RedialableTransport`, a desktop-local extension of the protocol's `Transport` mirroring the phone's, so every in-process test double stays a plain `Transport` and the guard `isRedialableTransport()` no-ops on it): resets the backoff ladder and dials now. Without `force` it only rescues a transport sitting in backoff; with `force` it abandons the existing socket first, open or mid-dial, detaching its handlers before closing it so a late close can neither null the successor nor arm a second reconnect. `BridgeSession` is the caller with proof (a spent presence budget on a silent socket); `powerMonitor` `resume` is the caller with proof from the OS; the client itself calls it when a send would cross the per-socket byte cap.
- **Dial watchdog** (`DIAL_TIMEOUT_MS`, 30s): a dial that reaches none of `onopen` / `onerror` / `onclose` is abandoned and joins the backoff ladder. The network stack bounds TCP and TLS on its own; this covers a black-holed upgrade reply, the one way a transport can sit in `connecting` for minutes, and nothing above the transport could rescue that state because every presence path skips a non-connected transport.
- **Per-socket byte cap** (`maxBytesPerSession`, default 256MB, reset on each open) as defense-in-depth against a runaway send loop on either end. Reaching it forces a redial onto a fresh socket rather than throwing. A throw left the socket open with every later frame over the cap too, after the session had already sealed the refused frame.
- **Accountless:** no Kangentic account/entitlement coupling in this client. Any such gate belongs only on the hosted relay's own connection-acceptance policy (open-core design - see [section 10 of the research doc](../../research/mobile-companion-app.md#10-monetization--licensing-open-core)); this client behaves identically against a self-hosted or Kangentic-hosted relay.
- `src/main/mobile-bridge/transport/transport-factory.ts` is the deliberate swap point: pairing service, bridge sessions, and the capability router only ever see the `Transport` interface (`packages/protocol/src/transport/transport.ts`). A future WebRTC data-channel implementation (Phase 4) slots in at `createTransport()` with nothing above it changing.

## Reading the log

The bridge writes its connection trace through `console.*`, which `src/main/diagnostics/log-mirror.ts`
persists to `.kangentic/logs/<date>.log`: `warn` lines on every build, `log` lines only with
Persist Console Logs on (the dev-build default). So the routine churn stays at `log` and every
diagnostic edge is at `warn`, and the close code is what splits them. Labels are a truncated
device id or `pairing`; no slot ids, no key material, no message content.

| Line | Level | Means |
|---|---|---|
| `[mobile-bridge/relay-client <label>] connected after N ms (via <stack>)` | log | A dial opened. `<stack>` is the WebSocket that dialed: `chromium` for Electron's `net.WebSocket`, `node` for Node's global. The dial-failed and dial-timed-out lines end with the same suffix, so a latency series stays comparable across a change of stack. |
| `... parked slot timed out (code 4408) after N s; redial in N ms` | log | The relay's park timeout recycling a slot whose phone is away: once a minute per absent device, forever. When the redial lands inside 2s, this line and the `connected after` line that follows it are the whole trace of the recycle: the device row stays `offline` and writes no transition line. On 2026-10-21, 81 of 1,574 recycles (5%) took longer, up to 25s, and still wrote the `offline -> reconnecting -> offline` pair. |
| `... closed: code=1006 reason="" clean=false after N s connected[, last relay ping N s ago \| , no relay ping seen]; redial in N ms` | warn | The relay's keepalive reaped this socket (a missed pong), or the TCP peer died. The line this whole trace exists for. On an undici socket the line names when the relay last pinged it (every 30 s on the hosted relay, read from undici's `undici:websocket:ping` diagnostics channel), or says `no relay ping seen`; a ping age past ~30 s means the relay's side had stopped reaching the desktop. |
| `... closed: code=4000 reason="peer_closed" ...` | warn | The phone's half left; the relay tore this half down with it. |
| `... closed: code=4409 reason="slot_busy" ...` | warn | A third peer on the slot, or this desktop's own stale socket still counted at the relay; the contention probe evicts the dead one within 2s. |
| `... dial failed: <network error>; redial in N ms (via <stack>)` | warn | A dial that never opened, with the network error folded in (DNS, TCP, TLS, proxy): Chromium's close reason (`Error in connection establishment: net::ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED`), or undici's error text on the default Node stack. Thirty minutes of these is the reading `redialNow` cannot help and the backoff ladder already covers. |
| `... dial timed out after 30 s; redial in N ms (via <stack>)` | warn | The watchdog: a dial that reached no handler at all. |
| `... node failed N dials in a row and chromium opened; dialing via chromium for the next 30 min` | warn | The fallback stack crossed a network undici could not (a TLS-inspecting firewall, or a PAC list that leads with `DIRECT` on a network whose direct path is blocked). A proxy listed first never reaches this line: the client dials Chromium outright for it. The client stays on Chromium for 30 minutes, then tries undici again. |
| `... per-socket byte cap (N bytes) reached; redialing` | warn | The socket carried `maxBytesPerSession` bytes since it opened. A forced redial follows. |
| `[mobile-bridge] device <id8> rekey held N frame(s) for N ms, released (<reason>)` | log / warn | Frames a rekey held for the phone's msg2. `established` (sealed under the new keys) and `dispose` log at `log`. `deadline`, `overflow` (the hold reached 8 MiB), `read-failed` and `send-failed` sealed them under the old keys after the phone may have switched, so they log at `warn` and are the line to look for next to a phone request that timed out. |
| `... forced redial (<reason>) from <state>[, last relay ping N s ago \| , no relay ping seen]` | warn | `redialNow({ force })` acting: the session's spent budget, a system resume on a session with no phone attached, or the per-socket byte cap. The ping clause (only from `connected`, undici sockets only, as on the close line) tells a dead path, where the pings stopped too, from a silent phone on a live one, where they kept arriving. |
| `[mobile-bridge] system resumed from sleep: redialed N, probed N, skipped N of N device session(s)` | log | The per-session resume decisions. |
| `[mobile-bridge] screen unlocked: probed N of N device session(s)` | log | Only when at least one probe actually left. |
| `[mobile-bridge] device <id8> <from> -> <to> (transport <state>)` | log / warn | Every change of the reported connection state: `-> connected` and `-> connecting` at `log`; `-> offline`, `-> reconnecting`, `-> closed` at `warn`. A reconnect shorter than `RECONNECT_GRACE_MS` (2s) changes nothing and writes nothing once the device has a presence verdict, so `-> reconnecting` then means the relay link was down for longer than that. Before its first verdict, a drop writes `-> reconnecting` at once. The `(transport <state>)` clause is the raw relay socket state, not the reported one, so an absent phone reads `connected -> offline (transport connected)`. |
| `[mobile-bridge] device <id8> handshake established` | log | A KK handshake completed. |
| `[mobile-bridge] device <id8> handshake failed: ...` | warn | A garbled or injected handshake frame; a fresh initiation follows in `HANDSHAKE_RETRY_MS`. |
| `[mobile-bridge] device <id8> rejected a frame: ...` | warn | A frame the session could not open, one line per 10s window per device with the suppressed count. |
| `[mobile-bridge] device <id8> forcing a redial: <reason>` | warn | The spent-budget verdict, from the session's side. |
| `[mobile-bridge] slow request <verb>[/<action>] <requestId> from <id8>: handler N ms, [seed N ms (settle N ms, serialize N ms), Nk chars,] [seed shrink N ms,] send N ms[, N kB frame][, N kB still buffered], longest main-loop block N ms` | warn | The desktop's share of one phone request reached 750 ms (`SLOW_REQUEST_WARN_MS`). The requestId is whole, to match the phone's own log of the same request. The main-loop block is the longest stretch main's event loop could not run a timer while the request was in flight: near the handler time means main was busy, near the ~12-20 ms timer floor means the time went to an await (the pty host, the disk). `handler` runs from the decoded frame to the handler's answer, and `seed` is the terminal seed inside it, split into the pty host's repaint settle and its serialize: fewer scrollback rows only help when the serialize dominates. `seed shrink` is present only when the full seed did not fit the wire and the handler re-took it with less history (see the shrink line below), and times those re-takes together. `send` is encode, seal and hand-off to the socket, and `still buffered` is the WebSocket's `bufferedAmount`. On undici that is only what the OS socket has not yet accepted, not what is still in flight: a 4 MB burst through the hosted relay read 0 within 30 ms of `send()` returning, and the receiver had it all only after 2.5 s (1.7 MB/s). So the clause shows up for a stalled socket, never for an ordinary backlog, and a desktop-side backpressure rule cannot be built on it. The relay's queue toward the phone, where a slow phone link actually backs up, is invisible from the desktop. The phone times the whole round trip, so whatever it measured beyond this line was spent on the wire or in the relay. |
| `[mobile-bridge] response to <verb>[/<action>] <requestId> from <id8> not sent: <reason>; answered with a refusal` | warn | A response over the protocol's frame caps (1 MiB compressed, 4 MiB before compression). The phone gets `{ ok: false, error: 'Response too large to send', code: 'response-too-large' }` instead of waiting out its timeout. The code says the request was valid and its target still exists, so a phone must not read it as the target being gone. This is the backstop for a verb that cannot send less. `read-stream` never reaches it: its terminal seed is shrunk to fit (see the next row). A handler that subscribed before it answered keeps that subscription, and `read-board` and `read-diff` subscribe first and rely on this refusal, since their measured responses are far under the caps. |
| `[mobile-bridge] read-stream/subscribe <requestId> from <id8> shrank its terminal seed to fit the wire: <N>k chars to <N>k chars (sent <N> history lines / sent the grid alone / sent an empty seed)` | warn | A `read-stream` terminal subscribe whose full seed made the response go over the frame caps. The handler re-took the seed with less history until the response fit, and answered `ok` with that seed, so the phone always gets a terminal. The first number is the full seed, the second the one sent, and the parenthesis names the rung that held. A shrunk seed is a shorter history above a whole grid, except the last rung, which sends no seed and leaves the phone's terminal blank until the next repaint. Measured alt-screen seeds are 2 to 3 KB, so this line means unusual output, such as per-cell truecolor across the whole grid and scrollback. |

## Wake sources

`src/main/index.ts` wires two `powerMonitor` events to the service, next to the existing
`suspend` heartbeat. `resume` calls `resumeAllSessions()`, and each session decides on its own
evidence (`BridgeSession.resumeFromSleep()`): a machine that slept comes back with every relay
socket reaped and nothing to tell the bridge, but `resume` also fires after a standby short
enough that the socket survived, and a forced redial on a socket a live phone is using costs
that phone a bounce on every wake. So a session with no phone attached (parked, or already
absent) abandons its socket and dials afresh at once (about 1s instead of the ~125s the rekey
tick would take, and nobody to bounce), while a session whose phone was present is probed and
only a spent budget (~10s) redials it. `unlock-screen` calls `probeAllPresence()`: an unlock is a
hint, not proof, so each session sends one guarded presence probe (`beginHandshake()` without
`replaceOutstanding`, a no-op on a parked slot with a msg1 already buffered, so it cannot recreate
the #635 double initiation) and lets its own budget decide. A healthy session pays one rekey; a
dead one fails the budget in ~10s and redials on the evidence. Both log what they actually did
(redialed / probed / skipped counts), and a quiet unlock with every phone away writes nothing.
Neither touches an in-flight pairing ceremony, which has its own timeouts. Electron emits
`unlock-screen` on Windows and macOS only; on Linux a zombie socket is recovered by `resume`,
the rekey tick, and the spent-budget redial alone.

## Honest relay-metadata statement

Even a correctly-implemented blind relay is not metadata-invisible. A relay operator (including a self-hoster, or Kangentic operating the hosted instance) can observe: source and destination IPs, connection timing, frame sizes and frequency, and the pairing graph (which slot ids co-occur). None of that is message *content* - the relay cannot decrypt anything - but it is real, observable metadata. Mitigations: self-hosting the relay, single-use pairing tokens, and the relay's own connection caps and per-IP/per-slot rate limits so only paired devices can consume relay capacity at all (implemented in `kangentic-relay`'s guards; see its README). This statement should stay in any user-facing security documentation rather than being implied away.
